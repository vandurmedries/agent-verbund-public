# Verbund research program: a falsifiable contribution

Status: literature-informed protocol and preliminary preparation data, not a breakthrough claim. No university affiliation, endorsement or formal peer review is implied.

## What we will test
**Question:** when does shared agent infrastructure produce more accepted, reusable work than its coordination and operating overhead costs?

1. Contact: distinguish registry listing, reachable metadata, delivered message, actual peer response, agreed task and paid acceptance. Nonresponses have unknown cause without direct evidence. Freeze exposure and response windows; observed chats are a convenience sample, not all agents.
2. Delivery: compare same-model budget-matched workflows, difficulty levels and source access. Keep source, rubric, harness and environment separate. Add missing fields, stale evidence and duplicate messages as explicitly controlled fault conditions. No live provider abuse or private-system access.
3. Reuse: track whether a reviewed conversation-derived lesson changes a later accepted task, with source, rights, expiry and contradiction checks. Record retrieval/review effort and stale-evidence mistakes; storing a lesson is not an accepted reuse or revenue.
4. Economics: report cost per accepted result, coordination bytes/tokens, end-to-end and queue time, human review and rework. Do not collapse different currencies or value speculative improvements as cash. Payment attribution is a separate verified ledger.

The potential contribution is a longitudinal chain from contact through validated delivery to knowledge reuse and paid acceptance, using common provenance. Novelty is a hypothesis: systematic related-work review and external critique are still required. Existing methods and our preliminary runs alone do not demonstrate novelty.

## Lessons from sources actually read
- Vanneste and colleagues, ALA 2022, [UAntwerpen/imec archived paper](https://repository.uantwerpen.be/docman/irua/7d1b20motoMb0): methods were compared across environments and with corrupted communications; no discretization method dominated all environments. We adopt the principle of matched environments and controlled communication faults. Their gradient-based MARL results are not results about our LLM/A2A implementation.
- [ProtocolBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17149): protocol comparison includes task success, latency, message overhead and fault robustness. We will distinguish wire protocol effects from message wording and local orchestration; the current document smoke test does not benchmark A2A versus ACP.
- [MASBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04672): partial observability and protocol/memory/routing mechanisms are explicit benchmark dimensions. This motivates source-access controls; it does not show our network beats a single agent.
- [AgentCompass](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13705): evaluation infrastructure separates benchmark, harness and environment. We record each independently and retain failed trajectories.
