# Positioning and pricing research

13 October 2026. Decisions below are hypotheses, not proven demand or live changes to existing offers.

## Initial positioning

For small AI builders and integrators with a reproducible agent/API handoff failure, Agent Verbund offers a bounded diagnostic: reproduce the failure, retain evidence and deliver a prioritized repair plan with acceptance checks. The network coordinates discovery, evidence and review. Claims of lower cost, faster completion or stronger quality require measured comparisons; worldwide partners and guaranteed outcomes are not part of the promise.

The purchase unit is one defined workflow and diagnostic result. Free tracing tools, doing the investigation internally, a small marketplace check and hiring a freelancer are alternatives. Buyers can keep their existing tools. Our differentiator to test is an actionable investigation and reusable test fixture across interfaces, rather than the existence of a dashboard or an agent network.

Priority 1: integrator/founder responsible for a broken workflow, with public/synthetic or explicitly authorized inputs and authority to purchase. Priority 2: delivery partners who need independent evidence before accepting integration work. Business process audits and generic research reports remain separate hypotheses: their scope, procurement and liability differ. Agent interest alone does not establish a budget owner.

## Market anchors and limitations

Our dated [market research](MARKET-RESEARCH.md) records Speedbot asking prices: 0.50 USDC for a public API check, 6 for a JSON comparison and 12 for code review. These are listed offers, not demonstrated sales or equivalent scopes. [LangSmith](https://www.langchain.com/pricing) and [Braintrust](https://www.braintrust.dev/pricing) offer free entry with quotas and paid plans; custom diagnostics must justify additional spending. [Hardy & Butler](https://hardyandbutler.ai/pricing.html) sells a scoped business audit, a different purchase unit. No competitor average is an optimal price.

## Proposed packaging to test

| Package | Defined result | Pricing status |
|---|---|---|
| Public example | Existing synthetic failure fixture and example report; no custom support commitment | Free discovery asset |
| Narrow check | One bounded, deterministic public-input check; explicit supported input and output limits | Existing 10-USDC proposal can be tested only if total delivery effort supports it |
| Workflow diagnostic | One workflow, bounded evidence bundle, reproducible failure/check, prioritized repair plan and one defined clarification round | Explore 35–60 USDC; not a quotation, live listing or validated willingness to pay |
| Implementation / partner delivery | Explicit change, acceptance test, responsibilities and dependency costs | Scope-specific quote after feasibility and capacity review |

A diagnostic can validly find no reproducible fault. Agree the evidence/report acceptance criteria rather than promise a repair or commercial gain. Do not silently increase an existing quoted price. Agree time window, data rights, cancellation/failure treatment and extra costs before an order. Publication as a purchasable service requires a working order/delivery route and reserved capacity.

## Cost floor: illustrative, not measured

Let C = compensated delivery + review + expected rework + variable infrastructure cost, F = fixed transaction cost, f = proportional platform/payment fee, and m = desired contribution margin fraction of selling price. Then P_min = (C + F) / (1 - f - m), provided the denominator is positive. Taxes and currency conversion must be treated explicitly in an actual quote; they are not estimated here. Partner compensation is a cost, not our revenue.

Illustration only: assumed labor value 30 USDC/hour, assumed proportional fee 5%, target contribution margin 30%, zero fixed fee and zero other variable costs. A 10-USDC price permits 6.50 USDC of total cost, or 13 minutes of combined delivery/review/rework. At 45 minutes, labor cost is 22.50 USDC and the floor is about 34.62 USDC. At 90 minutes it is about 69.23 USDC. These rates, fees and margins are assumptions; free local inference does not make labor, hardware or rework free. Thus 35–60 USDC may still be too low for a lengthy investigation.

Record actual active minutes by delivery/review/rework, elapsed time separately, calls/tokens, provider charges, fees, partner costs, refunds and accepted outcome. Publish neither savings nor margin until measured. Prefer contribution per qualified opportunity = purchase rate × expected contribution per order over revenue alone, with capacity as a constraint.

## Buyer research and price validation

1. Recruit actual budget owners with a recent relevant failure. Ask about their last purchase, current workaround, consequences, authority, required evidence and deadline before showing our price. Record sources and optional permission for commercial reuse. Keep peer referrals separate from buyers.
2. Show exactly the same deliverable at candidate prices. [Gabor–Granger](https://www.qualtrics.com/support/common-use-case/xm-solutions/pricing-study-gabor-granger/) asks purchase intention at stated prices; [Van Westendorp](https://www.qualtrics.com/articles/strategy-research/how-to-run-pricing-study/) explores perceived price acceptability. Both are stated preference, not a paid order. The earlier 10-USDC public question is anchored and must remain labelled separately.
3. Initially conduct 5–10 purposively selected buyer interviews as an exploratory goal, not a representative sample or statistical guarantee. Use consistent questions and retain refusals, no-budget and alternative-provider decisions. Avoid declaring an optimal price from a tiny convenience sample.
4. Once feasibility and capacity are established, test transparent, real fixed-scope quotes in small sequential cohorts. Keep scope constant, document allocation and timing, honor every quote and measure acceptance, funding, delivery, rework, refunds and verified payment. Nonrandom cohorts have time/selection confounding. No fake checkout or invented scarcity.
5. Choose a price only when it covers measured costs and buyers accept the result. Change scope or target segment when demand does not cover the cost floor. An unanswered conversation does not prove price rejection.
